# 9. Getting help

Sometimes things go wrong in ways the manual doesn't cover, or the
fix is something only the operator can do. This section is about how
to get help — quickly, and with the least back-and-forth.

## The fastest way: chat

**The chat inside Roman SMS is the fastest way to reach the
operator.** It's in the left menu, labelled **Chat**. Messages you
send there go directly to the operator and usually get a response
within a working day — sometimes within minutes if the operator is
at their computer.

Why chat is better than email or phone:

- **It's inside the app.** No need to look up contact details.
- **It's linked to your account.** The operator can see which
  organization you're from, which plan you're on, and what your
  recent activity looks like, without asking.
- **It's recorded.** Both you and the operator can refer back to
  what was said.
- **It reaches the operator even when they're not online.** The
  message stays in their queue.

Use email only if you need to attach something the chat can't handle
— an invoice PDF, a spreadsheet, a screenshot. Use phone only for
urgent issues that can't wait.

## What to include when you ask for help

The single biggest factor in how fast you get help is **how specific
your message is**. A good help request includes:

**1. What you were trying to do.**
"I tried to send a fee reminder to the Form 1 Parents group."

**2. What actually happened.**
"Nothing happened. The button is greyed out."

**3. The specific identifiers.**
Campaign number, contact number, schedule number, invoice number —
whatever is relevant. These show up in the URL when you're on the
page, or on the detail page itself.

**4. What you've already tried.**
"I refreshed the page. I logged out and back in. Still greyed out."

**5. A screenshot if it's a visual problem.**
Especially useful for "the page looks wrong" issues.

Compare:

**Not specific:** "My campaign didn't send. Please help."

**Specific:** "Campaign #245, scheduled for today at 8 AM, is
showing as 'pending' on the detail page. It's been over an hour. I
have credits and my plan is active (expiry 2026-12-01). 500
recipients in the Form 1 Parents group. Can you check what's
happening?"

The specific version is answerable in one message. The vague
version requires three or four back-and-forth messages just to
establish the basics. Same problem, but the specific version costs
five minutes instead of an hour.

## Before you ask: three quick checks

If something doesn't work, spend two minutes on these before
messaging the operator. Half of "it's broken" moments resolve in
this step.

**1. Reload the page.** A surprising number of issues — a button
that won't click, a count that looks wrong, a status that doesn't
update — resolve with a browser reload. Ctrl+R (or Cmd+R on Mac).

**2. Check your notifications.** The bell at the top of the
dashboard. If there's a pending action — an import with errors, a
plan expiring, a low balance — the notification usually tells you.

**3. Look at the dashboard.** The dashboard is deliberately built
to surface the most common problems before you notice them. Wallet
low? Plan expiring? Recent failed campaign? It's all there.

If none of these explains it, then message.

## What the operator can help with

The operator has access to the platform's backend — things you
can't do from your account. They can:

- **Reset passwords** — for you, or for any user in your
  organization. Useful when someone is locked out and you can't
  log in as the admin either.
- **Grant credits** — as an adjustment, refund, or bonus. If you
  believe you were charged incorrectly, this is how it gets fixed.
- **Change your brand prefix** — the short identifier at the
  start of every message. This is a master-only setting. The
  operator also uses it to set up your account when you first sign
  up.
- **Suspend or terminate your account in an emergency** — for
  example, if there's a legal or compliance issue. Rare.
- **Investigate billing discrepancies** — if your wallet shows a
  charge that doesn't match what you did, the operator can trace
  it.
- **Look at server-side logs** — if something failed on the
  platform's side (dispatch failure, provider error, network
  issue), the operator can see what happened and why.
- **Fix technical problems on your account** — if you're seeing
  errors on pages, or something is behaving in a way the manual
  doesn't explain.

## What the operator cannot do

Equally important — things the operator will tell you "no" to, and
why.

- **Undo a sent message.** Once a message leaves Roman SMS, it's
  delivered — SMS is immediate. The operator can't recall it.
- **Restore a deleted user.** Deleting a user is permanent. If the
  user's history matters, terminate rather than delete (see
  Section 7).
- **Reverse an opt-out.** A phone that opted out stays opted out.
  This is a legal requirement, not a platform policy. The operator
  can't override it.
- **See what you sent to a specific contact.** The operator can
  see that *a* campaign was sent and its delivery counts, but not
  the individual message body unless they have a specific reason
  to investigate (say, a compliance report). Your messages are
  your messages.
- **Add credits without an invoice.** If you bought a plan, you
  get credits. You can't receive a "free grant" without a
  corresponding invoice or a documented adjustment.
- **Change your message content after you've sent it.** Same as
  undoing a sent message.

If your question is "can the operator do X," and X is on this list
of can'ts, the answer is already no. Save yourself the wait and
think about the workaround. If you're not sure which list a
question belongs to, ask — but be prepared for the answer to be one
of the can'ts.

## Emergencies

An emergency is:

- **You cannot send messages and have a critical send due in the
  next few hours.**
- **Your account has been suspended and you don't know why.**
- **You believe you've been charged incorrectly and the amount
  matters.**
- **You suspect your account has been accessed by someone who
  shouldn't have access.**

For these, message the operator through chat and put "URGENT" in
the message. Urgent messages usually get a faster response.

For non-urgent questions, skip the "URGENT" tag. It's rare that a
flag actually saves you time, and it uses the operator's attention
in a way that slows response to real emergencies.

## A word about response times

The operator is a small team — sometimes one person. Response times
vary:

- **Chat message:** usually within a working day, often faster.
- **Urgent issues:** usually within an hour during business hours.
  Slower at night and on weekends.
- **Payment processing:** typically within hours of payment, but
  can take longer if the operator is away.

If you don't hear back in a full working day on a non-urgent
question, a gentle follow-up is fine. Do not send five messages in
an hour — that doesn't speed up the response, it just adds noise to
the operator's queue.

## Helping the operator help you

A few small habits that make support faster:

**Reply to the same thread.** When you message about an issue, wait
for a response in that same conversation. Don't start a new chat
about the same problem — it fragments the context.

**Answer questions directly.** If the operator asks "what's your
plan's expiry date," tell them the date. Don't say "it's the one I
bought last month." They can't see your whole account without
looking things up, and each lookup takes time.

**Be honest about what you've tried.** If you've already
restarted, re-imported, or retried, say so. It avoids the operator
suggesting steps you've already done.

**Don't assume the operator remembers your last conversation.** The
operator handles many accounts. Reference the specific issue,
campaign number, or invoice number even if you discussed it
recently.

**Close the loop.** If an issue is resolved, say so in the chat.
The operator might still be watching for a follow-up that isn't
coming.

## If chat isn't working

If the chat page is broken or won't load, you can reach the
operator by email or phone using the contact details on your
invoices — the invoice PDF has the operator's direct contact
information. This is uncommon; the chat usually works. But if it
doesn't, the invoice has an escape hatch.
